# A Little Girl with a Big Part

## Lesson Brief

- **Series:** Families in the Bible
- **Subject/Theme:** God works through the faith of parents and the willing help of children.
- **Bible Focus:** Amram and Jochebed protect Moses, Miriam helps bring him home, and Moses later chooses to serve God.
- **Main Passage:** Exodus 1:1-2:10; Hebrews 11:23-29. Background: Genesis 45:5-11; 46:1-7. Closing overview: Exodus 2:11-3:12; 12:37-42; 14; Deuteronomy 34:1-9.
- **Main Point:** God can use you now, so learn His Word and do the part He gives you with a willing heart.
- **Teaching Aim:** Each child will choose one way to help at home or obey a Bible truth this week.
- **Teaching Pace:** Keep the Egypt backstory brief. Spend most of the story time at the riverbank and on Miriam's offer. Give Moses' later life as a short closing overview.
- **Family Names:** Amram and Jochebed were the parents of Moses, Aaron, and Miriam (Exodus 6:20; Numbers 26:59). Aaron was three years older than Moses (Exodus 7:7). Miriam's exact age is not given.
- **Teacher Preparation:** Read [Exodus 2](https://biblehub.com/kjvs/exodus/2.htm), [Genesis 46](https://biblehub.com/kjv/genesis/46.htm), [Acts 7](https://biblehub.com/kjv/acts/7.htm), and [Hebrews 11](https://biblehub.com/kjv/hebrews/11.htm) in the KJV. Moses was hidden for three months. The basket was placed among reeds at the riverbank, where his sister watched. Patrols and encounters with boats or animals are outside the recorded account. Build suspense through the king's command and the princess discovering a Hebrew baby. Describe imagined feelings as possibilities. Jochebed's exact lessons and Moses' palace curriculum are also unrecorded; distinguish those inferences from the facts below.

## Lesson Outline

### 1. A Family Becomes a Nation in Egypt

A brief background:
- Joseph brought his family to Egypt to save them from a fame
- They stayed in Egypt instead of going home to Israel
- They grew in great number
- Pharoah turned them into slaves
- To keep them from rebelling, he started killing all baby boys

> Exodus 1:22
And Pharaoh charged all his people, saying, Every son that is born ye shall cast into the river, and every daughter ye shall save alive.

Pharaoh commanded all his people to cast every newborn Hebrew boy into the river. That means every person who finds, sees, hears a baby boy, was to take the baby and throw them in the river. Can you imagine?
- Imagine finding a baby. You now had to decide if you were going to kill the baby, or disobey and risk being killed yourself!
- Imagine having a baby brother, knowing at any time someone could find him and take him and throw him in the river!
- Imagine being a parent, have a sweet baby that you love so much, worried that anyone could find him and kill him!

### 2. Amram and Jochebed Trust God with Their Baby

> **Exodus 2:1-3**
> And there went a man of the house of Levi, and took to wife a daughter of Levi. And the woman conceived, and bare a son: and when she saw him that he was a goodly child, she hid him three months. And when she could not longer hide him, she took for him an ark of bulrushes, and daubed it with slime and with pitch, and put the child therein; and she laid it in the flags by the river’s brink.

**Summary/Lesson:** Amram and Jochebed had a baby boy. For three months they hid him. Babies need care through the day and night, and a parent cannot simply tell an infant to stay quiet. The Bible says they reached the point when they could hide him no longer. We are not told exactly what changed. Hebrews explains what guided their choice: faith in God was stronger than the king's command:

> **Hebrews 11:23**
> By faith Moses, when he was born, was hid three months of his parents, because they saw he was a proper child; and they were not afraid of the king’s commandment.

Jochebed made an ark of bulrushes, a small basket made from reeds. She coated it with slime and pitch to keep water out, placed Moses inside, and laid it among the reeds at the river's edge.

> Let the children picture her lowering the basket into its place. A small baby was now outside the house, near the water, under a law that threatened his life. His mother had taken care to protect him, yet she could not control what would happen next. His sister watched from a distance.

> **Psalms 56:3**
> What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee.

### 3. Miriam Watches as the Princess Finds the Basket

> **Exodus 2:4-6**
> And his sister stood afar off, to wit what would be done to him. And the daughter of Pharaoh came down to wash herself at the river; and her maidens walked along by the river’s side; and when she saw the ark among the flags, she sent her maid to fetch it. And when she had opened it, she saw the child: and, behold, the babe wept. And she had compassion on him, and said, This is one of the Hebrews’ children.

**Summary/Lesson:** His sister, Miriam, stood far enough away to watch what would happen. She had a part she could do: she could pay attention. Then Pharaoh's daughter came down to wash in the river while her maidens walked beside her. The princess saw the basket in the reeds and sent her maid to get it.

The basket was being opened by someone from the king's own family. What would she do when she saw the baby? Being the very daughter of Pharoah, it was her duty to kill the baby. But God works in the hearts of even the most powerful people. Remember, Miriam was watching afar off. Imagine what was going on in her mind: "Oh no! The princess has him! Surely she will kill him!"

But when the princess saw Moses, he cried, and she had compassion on him. She knew he was a Hebrew child. She knew what she was supposed to do, but she decided to save the baby

### 4. A Child Speaks Up, and Moses Goes Home

**Summary/Lesson:** God was leading Miriam to do something. She could see that the princess was going to save the baby, but she also knew that the princess wasn't going to take care of the baby. She didn't have the time, and probably didn't want to either. Miriam had a chance to go to the princess and say something. Of all the things she could say or do, what do you think it would be?

> **Exodus 2:7-9**
> Then said his sister to Pharaoh’s daughter, Shall I go and call to thee a nurse of the Hebrew women, that she may nurse the child for thee? And Pharaoh’s daughter said to her, Go. And the maid went and called the child’s mother. And Pharaoh’s daughter said unto her, Take this child away, and nurse it for me, and I will give thee thy wages. And the woman took the child, and nursed it.

She decided to offer to find a Hebrew woman who could care for and raise the baby. The princess agreed. Who do you think Miriam took the baby to? Her own mother! Not only would Moses live, but Jochabed would be able to keep him, at least for a while. And not only would she get to keep him, but she was going to be paid to do it!

Pharaoh's daughter placed Moses in his mother's care and promised to pay her. Miriam's watchful attention and timely words mattered. God used a little girl from an unprivileged family to change the life of her brother, the life of her mother, and the future of an entire nation. Image the joy on Jochabed's face when Miriam brought the baby home under the protection of royal guards!

### 5. Truth Learned at Home Can Stay for Life

> **Exodus 2:10**
> And the child grew, and she brought him unto Pharaoh’s daughter, and he became her son. And she called his name Moses: and she said, Because I drew him out of the water.

**Summary/Lesson:** Jochebed cared for Moses until the time came to bring him to Pharaoh's daughter. We don't know how old he was. We don't know how long she had Moses for. Her time was short and she knew she had to teach him wisely to stay true to God when he returned to the palace.

Jochabed didn't have this Bible verse yet, but she sure did trust in it:

> Proverbs 22:6
Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.

Moses was going to leave a home that worshipped and obeyed God to live with people who delighted in sin and worshipped evil, fake gods. But Jochabed knew that God had a plan for Moses. Moses was going to do something great for God. She just didn't know what yet.

### 6. Moses Chooses God in a Different World

> **Acts 7:22**
> And Moses was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians, and was mighty in words and in deeds.

**Summary/Lesson:** Moses became the son of Pharaoh's daughter. And we see that he learned the wisdom of the Egyptians. His life now included the royal household and an education shaped by Egypt. The nation worshipped false gods, and taught many things against God's Word.

> **Hebrews 11:24-26**
> By faith Moses, when he was come to years, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh’s daughter; Choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season; Esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt: for he had respect unto the recompence of the reward.

As Moses grew up, he remembered what his parents taught him. He could have chosen the riches, pleasures, power, and fame that came with being an Egyption prince, but instead, he chose to identify with God's people even though they were just slaves. The comforts and standing of the royal household could not give him the peace of obeying God. This does not mean every decision Moses made was right. Moses was a sinner too, but his heart was after God, not after the world.

### 7. God Uses the Baby Who Was Protected

**Summary/Lesson:** Moses would grow up to save the Hebrews from slavery. They would escape Egypt and God would use Moses to escape their great army, cross a sea, and face many mighty enemies as they travelled to the land that God had promised them. God had great plans for Moses, but they all started with a mother, father, and sister, who stuck together and made brave decisions to obey God instead of giving in to an evil king.

## Segue Into The Plan Of Salvation

Moses needed rescue when he was a helpless baby. Years later, God used him to lead Israel out of slavery. Those were real rescues from danger. The Bible also tells us that every one of us needs Jesus to save us from sin.

Sin is the wrong we do against God. Romans 3:23 tells us that everyone has sinned. We have disobeyed, lied, or treated someone cruelly. God is holy, and sin deserves His judgment. Helping at home cannot erase that sin. Being raised by faithful parents cannot give us their faith as our own.

God loves us and sent His Son, Jesus Christ. Jesus lived without sin, died for our sins, and rose again, as 1 Corinthians 15:3-4 teaches. Ephesians 2:8-9 explains that salvation is God's gift, received through faith. You can turn to Jesus and trust Him to save you. Acts 16:31 calls us to believe on the Lord Jesus Christ.

If you want to understand how to trust Jesus, speak with a teacher after the lesson. Those who already belong to Him can ask for help to serve Him willingly this week. Our service grows from His love and the new life He gives us.

## Main Applications For The Kids

- Ask God for courage when doing right feels hard.
- Pay attention when someone needs help, and involve a trusted adult when the situation needs one.
- Learn God's Word from those who teach it faithfully, then put it into practice.
- Follow God's truth when friends pressure you to do wrong.
- Choose one way to help your family or someone at church this week.
- Trust Jesus personally for salvation.
