# The Smallest Becomes the Greatest

## Lesson Brief

- **Series:** Families in the Bible
- **Subject/Theme:** Faithful obedience in small duties and a heart that trusts God.
- **Bible Focus:** God chooses David over his older brothers, David serves his father faithfully, and God uses him to defeat Goliath.
- **Main Passage:** 1 Samuel 16:1-23; 17:1-52.
- **Main Point:** God sees your heart. Obey and serve Him faithfully in the small things, and let Him use your life for His purpose.

## Memory Verse 
> **Colossians 3:23**
> And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men;

## Lesson Outline

Today we will talk about a family that had eight sons. Unlike last week, even though this family still believed and trusted in God, they were all kind and loving to each other. The brothers and even the father did not think much of one of the sons and treated him poorly.

We don't know much about the mom, but the dad's name was Jesse, and we do know he had 8 sons. And we know 7 of their names:
- Eliab
- Abinadab
- Shimma
- Nethaneel
- Raddai
- Ozem
- David

David was the youngest of the family. He wasn't treated very well by his brothers. He also wasn't treated very well by his own father. 

David's 3 oldest brothers served in the Israelite army, but David was stuck with the job of babysitting sheep alone, away from the family. He was alone for a great deal of time. He didn't get to build good relationships with his brothers or his parents. Since he was out with the sheep most of the time he spent his time doing 4 things:
- Watching over the sheep
- Practicing his slingshot
- Playing his harp
- Praying to God

David would grow to become a king some day, but before he does that, he has to learn how to serve and how to live in a world that didn't much like him.

### 1. David Was Faithful Before Anyone Called Him Great

**Summary/Lesson:** David was the youngest person in his family. As the youngest, he got one of the more boring jobs of the family, he was supposed to watch the sheep. He stood around a field watching sheep for hours, walking them here and there from field to stream and back. Sounds like a pretty boring job to me! certainly nothing too exciting.

![David diligently watches his sheep graze over rocky terrain.](image-1.png)

But David worked hard to take care of the sheep, even though it wasn't cool like the army. He even made sure the sheep were taken care of when he had to do other things. He didn't just tell his dad he was going to be gone, no, he got someone to take care of his work for him:

> **1 Samuel 17:20**
> And David rose up early in the morning, and left the sheep with a keeper, and took, and went, as Jesse had commanded him;

He even risked his own life to keep the sheep safe:

> **1 Samuel 17:34-35**
> And David said unto Saul, Thy servant kept his father’s sheep, and there came a lion, and a bear, and took a lamb out of the flock: And I went out after him, and smote him, and delivered it out of his mouth: and when he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, and smote him, and slew him.

David took his work seriously. It wasn't a joke or a game to him. His work was important to him even though it wasn't a big deal.

A lot of times, you and I might get a job that we don't care about. We don't work hard at it. Maybe we get distracted or lazy. I know I do. David didn't do that. He focused on the job and did it right.

> **Luke 16:10**
> He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much: and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much.

### 2. God Sends Samuel to Jesse's House

>> **1 Samuel 16:1**
> And the LORD said unto Samuel, How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? fill thine horn with oil, and go, I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite: for I have provided me a king among his sons.

 **1 Samuel 16:6-10**
> And it came to pass, when they were come, that he looked on Eliab, and said, Surely the LORD’S anointed is before him. But the LORD said unto Samuel, Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature; because I have refused him: for the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. Then Jesse called Abinadab, and made him pass before Samuel. And he said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this. Then Jesse made Shammah to pass by. And he said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this. Again, Jesse made seven of his sons to pass before Samuel. And Samuel said unto Jesse, The LORD hath not chosen these.

![Samuel and Jesse stand before Jesse's seven older sons. David is absent, and Samuel looks puzzled.](image-2.png)

**Summary/Lesson:** Eliab, Jesse's oldest son, came before Samuel. He looked like the kind of man people would expect to become king. He must have been tall, strong, and good looking! Samuel thought Eliab must be God's choice. God stopped Samuel and said, “Look not on his countenance, or on the height of his stature.” God did not say appearance has no value. He taught that a person's height, age, strength, clothing, talent, and place in a family do not tell us what is in that person's heart.

One by one, Jesse's seven sons passed before Samuel. God said no to each one. God had chosen someone else, but he wasn't there. Why wasn't David there? Was it because he was young? I don't think so. Other young brothers were there. Was it because he was serving? He was serving. He was watching the sheep. But I don't think that was the reason either. The older brothers had jobs too. Probably more important jobs. So why wasn't David there?

His father left him out on purpose. I don't think it was a mistake, I don't think Jesse forgot about David. I think Jesse just didn't think much about David and decided not to have him home when the prophet Samuel came to their home.

I don't know how you are treated. Maybe you are one of the cool kids, maybe you're not. I wasn't when I was a kid. Maybe you don't have many friends. Maybe you don't feel very loved at home. David knows how you feel. David would also tell you that just because you might feel alone in this world, that God is there for you. Even at this young age, David was a man after God's heart:

> **1 Samuel 13:14**
> But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and the LORD hath commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept that which the LORD commanded thee.

Even in the dark and loneliness, God was there with him.

### 3. David Knows God

> **1 Samuel 16:11-13**
> And Samuel said unto Jesse, Are here all thy children? And he said, There remaineth yet the youngest, and, behold, he keepeth the sheep. And Samuel said unto Jesse, Send and fetch him: for we will not sit down till he come hither. And he sent, and brought him in. Now he was ruddy, and withal of a beautiful countenance, and goodly to look to. And the LORD said, Arise, anoint him: for this is he. Then Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him in the midst of his brethren: and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward. So Samuel rose up, and went to Ramah.

![Samuel anoints kneeling David while Jesse looks surprised and the older brothers look upset.](image-3.png)

**Summary/Lesson:** Here we see David being chosen as the next king. And that's a great part of his life and story. Pretty awesome! But that's not what I want to focus on. Look at the middle part of verse 13:

> and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward.

Even though David didn't have the close brothers or father that he should have, he had God. And God would work such great things through David's life. Other than Jesus, more of the Bible is written about David than any other person. David is one of the most revered people in Israel's history. David, I'm sure wanted to be close to his brothers and father, but he put his strength in God and trusted in God, not his family.

People will fail you. Even the best people. We should still fogive and love them. But God will never fail you. David knew that.


### 4. David Stands up for God

Later, we find Israel in a battle and David's 3 oldest brothers are there. Many of you know the story, but there is a 10ft tall giant there cussing Israel and cussing God. All of the Israelite soldier's and even the king were too scared to attack because of the giant.

Jesse gave David some food to take to his brothers and their captain. When he got there, he saw the giant Goliath and heard him in his cussing fit. He started asking why no one was taking this guy out. He was cussing God and his country. Someone needed to take him out and end this battle. Well look at how the soldiers responded:

![David speaks earnestly with an angry Eliab among Israel's soldiers while Goliath shouts across the valley.](image-4.png)

> **1 Samuel 17:28-30**
> And Eliab his eldest brother heard when he spake unto the men; and Eliab’s anger was kindled against David, and he said, Why camest thou down hither? and with whom hast thou left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know thy pride, and the naughtiness of thine heart; for thou art come down that thou mightest see the battle. And David said, What have I now done? Is there not a cause? And he turned from him toward another, and spake after the same manner: and the people answered him again after the former manner.

It wasn't just his brother that was nasty to him. Everyone that David spoke to was ugly to him. David knew that someone needed to take a stand for what was right, and no one was. Everyone accused him of of being naughty, when everyone else was really just being a coward.

King Saul hears about this and calls for David. Now at this point, Saul already knew about David. David had served him in the palace by playing his harp. And Saul asks how David, as just a teenager, expected to defeated a highly trained killer like goliath. Look at David's response:

> **1 Samuel 17:37**
> David said moreover, The LORD that delivered me out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear, he will deliver me out of the hand of this Philistine. And Saul said unto David, Go, and the LORD be with thee.

David didn't say anything about his own skill or his own ability. He was putting all his faith and trust in God!

Boys and girls, it doesn't matter who you are, what skills you have, or how much other people like you. If you put your faith and trust in God, if you obey Him and seek to please Him, God will use you and greatly bless your life. Just follow and obey God like David did.

### 5. God Uses a Faithful Heart for His Purpose

**Summary/Lesson:** God may not ask a child to defeat a giant or become a king. He does call each child to obey, serve, learn His Word, and trust Him with a willing heart. Do not compare your part with another person's part. Faithfully do what God puts before you today, and leave the results and your future in His hands.

And do not let yourself think that you cannot serve or do great things for God just because you are little or can't do much. Don't think that God only uses adults. God wants to use you too! And not later when you are grown. He wants to use you now, today, this week!

## Segue Into The Plan Of Salvation

David was chosen by God and used by God, but David was still a sinner. He could not save himself or us from sin. Goliath was a terrible enemy, but the greatest enemy every person faces is sin. Sin is every thought, word, and action that disobeys God. Romans 3:23 says that all have sinned.

Good behavior cannot remove our sin. Helping at home, obeying parents, and serving at church are good things, but they cannot make us right with God. God sent Jesus Christ, the perfect King. Jesus never sinned. He died on the cross for our sins, was buried, and rose again the third day. First Corinthians 15:3-4 tells us this good news.

Jesus defeated sin and death through His death and resurrection. He alone can save us. Romans 10:13 says, “For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.” If you want to understand how to trust Jesus, speak with a teacher after the lesson. Those who have trusted Him can serve Him with joyful, faithful hearts.

## Main Applications For The Kids

- Ask God to give you a heart that loves and obeys Him.
- Stop comparing yourself with siblings, classmates, or friends.
- Serve faithfully in small tasks at home, school, and church.
- Obey parents and trusted leaders when their instruction agrees with God's Word.
- Trust God's power when doing right feels hard or frightening.
- Trust Jesus personally for salvation, because good works cannot save you.
