# Families in the Bible

## General Direction

This series explores families in the Bible and what children can learn from their choices. Each story connects a family relationship with choices children face at home or with other people.

Some lessons show how sin harms a household. Others show how God works through faithful people and uses family members to help each other do right. Keep each application tied to the Bible story and give children a clear way to act on it.

“Families in the Bible” is a working title. Other title ideas include “All in the Family” and “Family Matters.”

## Possible Lessons

The first selected lesson is [A Little Girl with a Big Part](01%20Everyone%20Has%20a%20Part/lesson-outline.md), about Moses' family. The remaining stories are options, with selection and teaching order still open. No dates or weeks are assigned.

## Notes for Developing Lessons

- Keep Cain's lesson focused on jealousy growing within a person's heart and the warning God gives before Cain acts. Joseph's lesson can explore jealousy between siblings and the damage it spreads through a family.
- Esau and Jacob's reunion can revisit the family division in “A Family Taking Sides” and show what happens when the brothers meet years later.
- Introduce the prodigal son as a parable Jesus told. Include the older brother's response when applying the story to family relationships.
- For Achan, keep the household central when explaining the consequences. The passage names his sons and daughters, but does not explain what each family member knew about his theft. Stay with what the text says when developing the lesson.
- Mordecai was Esther's older cousin and raised her as his daughter. Explain “positive peer pressure” as good influence from others, then apply it to friends as well as family.
- Show the specific ways Ruth's faithfulness was rewarded in her story. Avoid turning those events into a promise that every faithful person will receive marriage, children, or wealth.
- Give children responsibility for their own choices. Family problems can require help from a trusted adult; children should not carry the burden of repairing their parents' relationship. Obedience to God must guide any application about obeying a parent.
- The themes for Jesse and David, Joseph and Mary, Hannah and Samuel, and Elizabeth and John remain starting ideas that can be developed when lessons are selected.

## Series Thread

- Our choices affect the people who share our home.
- God calls us to take responsibility for our sin and respond to His correction.
- Jealousy and favoritism damage family relationships.
- Family members can encourage each other to obey God and act with courage.
- Forgiveness opens a way toward peace after family hurt.
- Children can take part in helping their family.
- God sees faithful love and can bring good through it.
- Each child can follow God within their own family circumstances.
