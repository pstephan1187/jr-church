# The Midnight Basket Escape

## Lesson Brief

- **Series:** Adventures in the Bible
- **Subject/Theme:** Boldly following Christ and trusting God when obedience brings danger
- **Bible Focus:** Saul preaches Christ in Damascus, becomes the target of a deadly plot, and escapes through the city wall with help from fellow believers.
- **Main Passage:** Acts 9:19-31
- **Main Point:** Jesus changes His people to live and speak boldly for Him, and when obedience brings danger, we can trust God to help us.

## Lesson Outline

### 1. The Man Who Came To Arrest Christians Joined Them

> **Acts 9:19-20**
> And when he had received meat, he was strengthened. Then was Saul certain days with the disciples which were at Damascus. And straightway he preached Christ in the synagogues, that he is the Son of God.

**Summary/Lesson:** Saul had traveled toward Damascus planning to arrest and kill Christians. Then Jesus stopped him on the road with a bright light. Saul entered the city blind and had to be led by the hand. God sent Ananias to him, Saul received his sight, and the feared enemy was now "Brother Saul."

Now Saul was spending time with the very Christians he had intended to capture. He immediately started telling people about Jesus and preaching in the synagogues. The change in his life was immediate and huge. Saul did not merely say that he had changed. He didn't keep doing all the things he was doing before. His life was completely different now! He stopped hunting Christians, he stopped killing people, he ended his exceeding anger, he started preaching, he started telling other about Jesus, he changed his friends. Jesus had completely changed his life.

> **2 Corinthians 5:17**
>
> Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

**Practical Application for Children:** Being saved does not mean that you become perfect overnight, but Jesus should begin changing what you enjoy, the way you speak to others, how you act, and the choices you make. A child who knows Christ can confess sin, obey parents, show kindness, choose better friends, and invite people to church. Good behavior cannot save us, but a saved life start making better choices.

### 2. Saul Boldly Proved That Jesus Is The Christ

> **Acts 9:21-22**
> But all that heard him were amazed, and said; Is not this he that destroyed them which called on this name in Jerusalem, and came hither for that intent, that he might bring them bound unto the chief priests? But Saul increased the more in strength, and confounded the Jews which dwelt at Damascus, proving that this is very Christ.

**Summary/Lesson:** The people who heard Saul were amazed. They asked whether this was really the same man who had arrested and killed Christians in Jerusalem and had come to Damascus to arrest more of them. It was the same Saul, **but he no longer served the same master.**

Saul "increased the more in strength" and proved from the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ. He did not hide his faith because someone might remember his past or laugh at his sudden change. He knew that Jesus was alive, that Jesus had saved him, and that people needed to hear the truth.

> **Romans 1:16**
> For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek.

**Practical Application for Children:** Christian boldness is not being loud, rude, or eager to argue. It is doing and saying what is right because Jesus is Lord, even when you feel nervous or scared. A child can bow his head to pray, refuse to join sinful behavior, invite a friend to church, or tell someone how Christ saves sinners. Ask God for courage, speak truthfully, and treat people with love.

### 3. The Former Hunter Became The Hunted

> **Acts 9:23**
> And after that many days were fulfilled, the Jews took counsel to kill him:

> **2 Corinthians 11:32**
> In Damascus the governor under Aretas the king kept the city of the Damascenes with a garrison, desirous to apprehend me:

**Summary/Lesson:** The Bible says that "after that many days were fulfilled," the Jews took counsel to kill Saul. The man who had once hunted Christians was now being hunted himself! Saul's obedience did not make everyone applaud him. The same people he used to work with to arrest and kill Christians were now super angry at him. They wanted him dead!

That the governor of Damascus guarded every gate because he wanted to capture Saul. This wasn't just someone who was going to give him a fine or make him do extra work. He was a very powerful man, and now Saul's life was in danger. Following Jesus had brought great change to his life, and his bold witness now brought great danger.

> **1 Peter 4:14**
> If ye be reproached for the name of Christ, happy are ye; for the spirit of glory and of God resteth upon you: on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified.

> **1 Peter 4:16**
> Yet if any man suffer as a Christian, let him not be ashamed; but let him glorify God on this behalf.

**Practical Application for Children:** Obeying Jesus can cost something. Other children may laugh when you refuse to cheat, exclude you when you will not join in making fun of someone, or call you strange because you believe the Bible. In some places, Christians face prison or death. We must decide that Christ is worth following even when it's dangerous.

### 4. Every Gate Was Watched Day And Night

> **Acts 9:24**
> But their laying await was known of Saul. And they watched the gates day and night to kill him.

> **2 Corinthians 11:32**
> In Damascus the governor under Aretas the king kept the city of the Damascenes with a garrison, desirous to apprehend me:

**Summary/Lesson:** Saul found out about the governor's plan, but escaping Damascus would not be easy. The city was surrounded by a great wall, and his enemies watched the gates "day and night" so they could kill him. Ask the children to picture each gate with guards staring at every traveler. Morning passed, but the guards stayed. Night fell, but the guards stayed. Every ordinary road out of the city appeared closed.

Even though it seemed like Saul was going to die, God had a plan. God knew the plot, allowed it to be discovered, and placed other believers around Saul. God did not make the walls disappear or strike every guard asleep. His help would come in a surprising way.

**Practical Application for Children:** Trusting God does not mean pretending a problem is small. Tell God exactly what frightens you, remember what His Word says, and seek help from a trustworthy parent, teacher, pastor, or other responsible adult. God often protects and guides us through wise plans and people He has placed in our lives.

**Illustration:** Choose several children to stand as "gates" around an open space while another child looks for an ordinary way out. Do not reveal the basket plan yet. Let the class feel the question: "If every gate is watched, how can Saul escape?"

### 5. Down The Wall In A Basket

> **Acts 9:25**
> Then the disciples took him by night, and let him down by the wall in a basket.

> **2 Corinthians 11:33**
> And through a window in a basket was I let down by the wall, and escaped his hands.

**Summary/Lesson:** When night came, the disciples took Saul to an opening in the wall. Houses were sometimes built against a city wall, with windows looking outside it. The guards were watching the gates below, but Saul would not use a gate. His new friends placed him in a large basket and carefully lowered him through a window and down the outside of the wall.

Saul hangs against the high wall while enemies watch for him nearby. He has to be quiet too! If he slips and cries out, the guards will hear him and know what's going on. The disciples above must be strong, and Saul must trust them enough to get in the basket. If they slip, he would fall about 30 feet to the ground and probably die. Lower and lower he goes until at last the basket reaches the ground. Saul steps out into the darkness and escapes Damascus alive. The guards had watched every gate, but God provided another way out.

### 6. God Preserved Saul To Keep Serving

**Main Passage:** Acts 9:26-31

**Summary/Lesson:** Saul would go on to do some incredible things for God! He would preach to a great many people and see a lot of them get saved. God did some miracles through Saul as he continued to live for and serve God.  He would help pastors and churches, he would write letters, he would heal people. All of that because He decided to turn away from his old life and turn to God.

## Segue Into The Plan Of Salvation

The people of Damascus could see that Saul's life had changed, but his bold preaching and dangerous escape were not what saved him. The change came because Saul had first met the risen Jesus and believed on Him. Good works are the result of salvation, not the price of salvation.

Every one of us has sinned against God. Romans 3:23 says, "For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God." You may never have hunted Christians as Saul did, but lying, disobeying, selfishness, anger, and every other sin still make you guilty before a holy God. You cannot escape sin by your own clever plan, and you cannot change your own heart.

Jesus, the sinless Son of God, died on the cross for our sins, was buried, and rose again. Saul could boldly preach that Jesus is the Son of God because he had met the living Christ. Jesus paid the full price for our sin and has power to give everlasting life to everyone who believes on Him.

Acts 16:31 says, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved." Do not try to earn salvation by acting brave, going to church, or doing good things. Admit that you are a sinner and place your trust in Jesus Christ, who died and rose again to save you. Then the same Savior who changed Saul will begin changing your life from the inside out.

## Main Applications For The Kids

- **Let salvation produce a visible change.** Good works cannot save you, but if Jesus has saved you, choose to obey Him in your words, friendships, attitudes, and actions.
- **Be bold with both truth and love.** Pray for courage to speak about Christ, refuse sin, and stand for what is right without becoming rude or reckless.
- **Expect that doing right may cost something.** Do not assume that teasing, exclusion, or trouble means you made the wrong choice when you obeyed God's Word.
- **Trust God when you are afraid.** Tell Him about the danger, remember His promises, use wise help, and leave the outcome with Him.
- **Hold the rope for someone else.** Pray for, encourage, protect, and practically help believers who are serving Christ or facing difficulty.

15. Medium: What should a child do when a problem is frightening or dangerous?
